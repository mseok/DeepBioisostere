Swap a fragment for an analogous one and you can tune a molecule's properties without losing its activity. Doing it in silico has meant telling the model where to cut and settling for one property at a time. DeepBioisostere chooses the site itself and steers several properties at once.

How it works

One forward pass makes three decisions. Nothing is supplied by an expert, and no substitution table is consulted.

1

Where to cut

The molecule is decomposed along BRICS bonds and every resulting subgraph is scored as a removal candidate. The model picks the site; you never mark one.

→
2

What to put back

The vacated site is matched against a library of 140,096 insertion fragments, scored jointly with the removal choice and with the property target as conditioning.

→
3

How to attach it

A fragment can often be joined in more than one orientation. The model scores the attachment points too, then composes the product molecule.

Conditioning is on the change, not the value. The model is told “lower logP by 1” rather than “reach logP 2.5”, so a single checkpoint serves any starting molecule, and several targets can be commanded at once. Training pairs come from experimental bioassay data, which is what lets the model propose replacements no substitution table lists.

Result 1

Two properties, steered at once

Three input molecules, each given the same pair of opposing commands — hold Mw, move logP by −1 and by +1. The black dot is the input; each contour set is the generated population under one command. The two populations separate along logP while both stay stacked over the input's molecular weight, and the model picks its own cut site in each molecule (highlighted above each plot).

The target decides where it cuts

Molecule 1 again, under each of the two commands. The bold atoms are the fragment the model chose to remove — and it is not the same fragment.

Removing the lipophilic group lowers logP and removing the polar one raises it, so the site the model picks tracks the direction it was asked to move — the choice of where to edit is part of what the conditioning controls, not a fixed preprocessing step.

Result 2

The choice depends on the environment

A control that isolates context. Two molecules, A and B, share the same leaving fragment. The model's own outputs for each (M′A, M′B) are compared against a graft (M″B): the fragments it chose for A, transplanted onto B's scaffold by BRICS. The transplant is where the two properties come apart.

100 generated insertions per molecule per target. QED is a whole-molecule property, so a fragment that improves it in one scaffold need not improve it in another — and the gap between the model's own choice and the transplant is the part that a lookup keyed on the leaving fragment could not supply. The model is reading the whole molecular graph, not just the fragment it is about to remove.

Result 3

Replacements beyond the known rules

One molecule, one fixed leaving fragment, 100 generated replacements. The proposals are chemically diverse and are not enumerations of a substitution table — several do not appear in the training pairs at all.

Case study

Hit-to-lead, on someone else's hits

The inputs here are raw outputs of a structure-based generative model — the kind of hit that scores well against a pocket but is awkward to make. DeepBioisostere is applied downstream. In both cases synthetic accessibility and drug-likeness improve together, and the docking score does not pay for it.

Lower SA is easier to synthesize, higher QED is more drug-like, and a more negative docking score is a better predicted pose — so every arrow above points the right way. These are the two cases shown in the paper; the docking provenance is archived with the record.

Try it

No conda environment and no compiled PyG extensions:

pip install deepbioisostere

Optimize a molecule for two properties at once:

deepbioisostere generate \
  --smiles "Cc1ccc2cnc(N(C)CCc3ccccn3)nc2c1" \
  --target mw=0 --target logp=-1 \
  --output results.csv

Or from Python:

from deepbioisostere import Conditioner, DeepBioisostere, Generator

properties = ["logp", "mw"]
model = DeepBioisostere.from_pretrained(properties=properties)
generator = Generator(
    model=model,
    conditioner=Conditioner(phase="generation", properties=properties),
    properties=properties,
)
df = generator.generate([("Cc1ccc2cnc(N(C)CCc3ccccn3)nc2c1", {"mw": 0, "logp": -1})])

Weights and the fragment library download and cache on first use. Every figure above is reproducible from the Zenodo record, which ships the analysis notebooks, the per-figure source data and the reproduction scripts.
